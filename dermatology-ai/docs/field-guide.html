01

The queue, not the answer

At eight in the morning a nurse does not diagnose. She decides who the dermatologist sees first. That decision is the product.

A diagnosis ends an argument. A triage decision starts one: see this one today, review that one in two weeks, this one is routine. It is also the thing a clinic can act on when there are twenty patients and one dermatologist.

So the machine sorts. It puts a case in one of three lanes — routine, follow-up in a short interval, or urgent — and it explains itself with a heat map. The clinician keeps the decision, and can overrule it in one click. That is why the study measures override rate, confidence shift and time to decide, not "accuracy".

Same model, two very different claims. The left-hand claim needs evidence this project does not have; the right-hand one is what the vignette study actually tests.

If the machine is wrong about a lane, the next clinician catches it. If a prototype is wrong about a diagnosis and nobody checks, the patient carries it home.

That asymmetry is the whole design brief. It is also why the project keeps its triage logic everywhere, rather than amputating it to make a cleaner demo.

02

The shelf is lopsided

The training shelf holds 2,298 phone photographs from Brazil. What it does not hold matters more than what it does.

PAD-UFES-20 is a real, open, downloadable dataset: 2,298 smartphone images, 1,373 patients, 1,641 lesions, every cancer confirmed by biopsy, and a Fitzpatrick skin type for each patient. It was chosen because it can be fetched today, not because it is perfect.

Look at the shape of it. One class is plentiful, one is scarce, and three are the wrong kind of cancer for this project.

Tap a bar. Counts are the published PAD-UFES-20 figures (CC BY 4.0); they are dataset facts, not model results.

The awkward part is the decision about what counts as "benign". Nevus and seborrhoeic keratosis are benign. Basal-cell and squamous-cell carcinomas are malignant — and not the classes this model handles. Folding them into "benign" would teach the system to call a real cancer reassuring, so they are excluded from training and named in the model card as an out-of-scope risk.

Fifty-two melanomas is not a dataset. It is a warning label that comes with a dataset.

With n that small, a single split can swing the result. That is why every melanoma number is reported with its interval — and why the failure cases are published, not buried.

03

A split is a promise

If the same person appears in training and in the test set, you are not measuring the model. You are measuring its memory.

Dermatology data arrives per lesion, but people have several lesions. Split by image and a patient's mole on the left arm trains the model while their mole on the right arm is used to grade it. The model scores beautifully and has learned the patient, not the disease.

The fix costs one line of thinking and no money: split by patient. Everyone is either in training or in evaluation, never both. A test then guards the promise so a future refactor cannot quietly break it.

04

Show your homework

A confident label is not an explanation. A heat map is not an explanation either — unless you look at what it is heating.

The model can be right for the wrong reason: a ruler in the frame, a pen mark, a tattoo. Those shortcuts are invisible in a score and obvious in a picture. So every prototype output carries a Grad-CAM overlay showing which pixels moved the decision.

And because a flattering gallery is easy to curate, the project commits to publishing the Failures Museum: twelve errors with their heat maps and one sentence each on why they happened. It is the most persuasive honesty artefact you can put in front of a clinician examiner.

05

The ledger, not the score

"Fair" is not a number you compute once. It is a table you publish, including the row where you lose.

The evaluation is stratified by skin tone: each Fitzpatrick stratum gets its own row, with its own n and its own confidence interval. If a stratum has no cases, it is absent from the table — never a tidy zero, which would read as "no errors".

Small strata produce wide intervals. That is arithmetic, not opinion — and seeing it is the fastest way to understand why a single headline accuracy figure is close to useless on this data.

So the deliverable is the Tone Gap Ledger: rows, intervals, and a sentence in words about the worst stratum. If the gap is large, that becomes the finding — it does not get tuned away.

06

The locked door

Kaposi sarcoma matters most in exactly the population this model cannot describe. There is no honest shortcut, so there is a gate.

KS is a defining AIDS-related cancer in the region, and it appears in none of the immediately downloadable public dermatology datasets. Training a third class on demonstration images would produce a checkpoint that looks complete and means nothing. So on 2026-10-31 the gate closed: no KS class until a real cohort exists, with serving disabled in code so nothing can quietly ship.

07

Twelve weeks, five hard dates

A plan is only real if it has dates you can miss.

Work runs on two lanes. Lane A is buildable today: fetch the data, train, freeze, evaluate across tones, run the clinician vignette study, write it up. Lane B is the KS cohort, gated on partners. Lane A ships even if Lane B never lands — that is the single most important decision in the plan.

    What it cannot do

    The shortest honest summary of the project.

    Five things this prototype will never claim

    • No Kaposi sarcoma class. Paused until a real cohort exists; serving is disabled in code.
    • No African cohort. Training data is Brazilian; that limitation goes in the abstract, not a footnote.
    • No clinical validity. It is a triage-ordering prototype under study, not a diagnostic device.
    • No performance figure yet. Every accuracy, sensitivity and fairness number reads not yet measured until a frozen manifest and its evaluation report exist.
    • No patient data. Synthetic and de-identified only, under the retention policy in force.

    What it can claim: a reproducible pipeline, a tone-stratified evaluation that publishes its worst row, a museum of its own failures, a frozen artefact whose hash you can verify, and a twelve-week plan where every claim traces to a file.
