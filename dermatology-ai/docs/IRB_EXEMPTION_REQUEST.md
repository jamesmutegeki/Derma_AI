## 2. What the study does

1. Train and evaluate an image classifier that sorts dermatoscopic images into
   three coarse groups -- benign, melanoma, Kaposi sarcoma -- using **public,
   already-published, fully de-identified datasets** (section 3).
2. Evaluate model behaviour across skin-tone groups where the source dataset
   provides a skin-type label.
3. Study a simulated clinician-in-the-loop workflow: a contextual-bandit triage
   policy decides ordering/urgency, and the effect of simulated clinician
   feedback on the policy is measured offline.
4. Report limitations, including that the system is a research prototype.

**No human participants are recruited. No human subjects are interacted with.
No identifiable private information is obtained. No biospecimens are used. No
new images are collected.** The clinician-in-the-loop component is evaluated
against logged, simulated feedback, not against real clinicians treating real
patients.

## 4. Why this is not human-subjects research

- The unit of analysis is an image and a model output, not a person. No
  participant is enrolled, consented, contacted or observed.
- The datasets are already public and already de-identified by their providers.
  The project receives no direct identifiers and makes no attempt to
  re-identify anyone; re-identification is prohibited by the SCIN and DDI terms
  and by this project's own policy (`SECURITY.md` §4).
- No intervention, no specimen, no medical record, no identifiable private
  information is obtained through interaction or observation.
- The system is not used to make or support a decision about any real patient:
  the API's diagnosis endpoint refuses to answer unless a frozen, hash-verified
  checkpoint is present, and its use with real patient images is prohibited
  until full approval exists.

**Determination requested:** that the project is exempt / does not constitute
human-subjects research, and that no further ethics review is required while the
scope above holds.

## 5. If the scope changes

The following would take the project outside this request and require a fresh
submission:

1. obtaining or using any real patient image or record, including from a partner
   clinic or a Ugandan cohort;
2. recruiting clinicians or students as study participants rather than
   simulating feedback;
3. deploying the system anywhere it could influence a real clinical decision;
4. linking any dataset to another dataset in a way that could identify a person.

Any of these is a stop-and-resubmit event, not an amendment to be assumed.

## 6. Data protection and security measures

- No patient data is held: the prototype runs on synthetic demo records, and the
  upload path is used with test images only.
- Retention and backup are governed by `SECURITY.md` §4, in force from
  2026-10-30, enforced by `scripts/purge_records.py` and
  `scripts/backup_db.py --verify`.
- Access is authenticated (Bearer tokens, revocable sessions), role-separated,
  and every destructive route is admin-only and audited.
- Logs contain no identifiers, tokens or password material.
- No dataset is committed to the repository; `.gitignore` and the
  definition-of-done gate both reject committed data.

## 7. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Results are read as clinical evidence | Research/educational-only, not-a-medical-device disclaimer on every artefact; no accuracy quoted without a frozen manifest and an evaluation report |
| Skin-tone bias is understated | Fairness claims restricted to datasets that actually carry tone labels; gaps are reported as "not yet measured" until measured |
| Re-identification of a public dataset subject | No linkage, no re-identification attempt, exports de-identified; terms of each dataset prohibit it |
| Model misclassification misleads a user | `/api/diagnose` returns 503 without a verified checkpoint; output is labelled a triage prompt, never a diagnosis |
